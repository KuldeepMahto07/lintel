About

Quiet support over every opening.

A lintel is the beam above a door or window. You rarely notice it, but it carries the load so the opening can do its job. That's the job we want software to do in a design office.

Why it exists

Design software does what it's told. Nothing more.

Architects lose hours every week to checks and edits that follow simple rules: door widths, stair geometry, room sizes, naming conventions. The tools are powerful, but they never look back at you and say "that door's 50 mm short".

Lintel is that second look. It never takes the pen out of your hand: it marks the drawing up and waits.

What we hold ourselves to

Four rules for our own software.

rule 01

Ask first

No change reaches a model without a person approving it. The assistant can draft; it can't apply.

rule 02

Show the work

Every flag cites its rule and reference. Every change is shown before and after.

rule 03

Keep the record

Everything is logged and everything can be undone. Your QA trail builds itself.

rule 04

Respect the code

We cite sections and never reproduce code text. Your adopted edition is the authority, not us.
