How Lintel works

It reads, it marks up, it waits for you.

Lintel works like a careful colleague with a red pen: it checks the drawing and marks it up, but your hand is on the stamp.

01

Read

The plugin reads rooms, walls, doors and stairs from the open model: names, uses, widths, areas, risers and treads.

02

Mark up

Every rule runs on every change. Problems appear as revision clouds with the requirement and the code section your rule cites.

03

Propose

For each fixable issue Lintel drafts the edit as a before-and-after diff. Geometry calls, like moving a wall, stay with you.

04

You approve

Approve and the edit lands in your model as one undo step. Reject and nothing changes. Either way it goes in the log.

05

Remember

Tell it "we always use 900 mm doors" and it proposes that as a firm rule. Once you confirm it, every project gets checked against it.

Your rules, your references

A rule is a number and where it came from.

Lintel doesn't ship anyone's code book. A rule is a requirement your firm sets (a minimum, a maximum, a travel distance) plus the section you checked it against. Import your office standards from a spreadsheet, adjust them per project, and check them against the edition your jurisdiction adopted.

name      Corridor clear width
applies   room where use = corridor
check     clear_width_mm ≥ 1118
reference IBC §1020 · verify adopted edition
fix       advice only (a wall move is your call)
